# T.R.A.C.K Research Guidelines

Use these rules before rewriting or editing any T.R.A.C.K job card. The goal is to make every description, responsibility list, career path, program listing, and certification listing accurate enough to be useful without making rankings, guarantees, or editorial claims.

## Research Standard

- Research every job card before changing it.
- Do not rely on memory when checking job duties, titles, programs, certifications, or career paths.
- Use current sources whenever the information could have changed.
- Cross-check important claims against more than one source when possible.
- Treat school and certification pages as stronger evidence than blogs, ads, forum comments, or third-party summaries.
- Do not add a program, certification, or career-development resource unless the source clearly supports the fit.
- Do not remove a program only because the title sounds imperfect. Check the course description, degree requirements, department page, and career outcomes first.
- If a program is adjacent but not directly focused, describe the fit honestly.
- If a job is usually a responsibility inside a larger role, consolidate it instead of keeping a separate card.
- If a title is real but rarely used, use the clearer current title and preserve the old title as a search alias when helpful.

## Valid Source Types

Prefer these sources:

- Official school program pages
- Official course catalogs
- Official department pages
- Official certificate or credential pages
- Accreditation or licensing body pages
- Union, guild, or professional association pages
- Government labor, licensing, or occupational pages
- Real job postings from employers, venues, studios, labels, production companies, universities, hospitals, manufacturers, or arts organizations
- Career pages from recognized industry organizations
- Official program handbooks or PDFs from schools and credential bodies

Use these sources only as supporting context, not as the main proof:

- Student forums
- Reddit threads
- Alumni interviews
- News articles
- Magazine lists
- YouTube descriptions
- Blog posts
- Ranking pages
- Third-party school directories

Do not use these as proof:

- AI-generated summaries
- Unsourced career pages
- SEO school-list pages with no program detail
- Ads that do not link to a real program
- Outdated archived pages unless the program still exists elsewhere
- Vague marketing pages that do not name a degree, certificate, curriculum, credential, or training route

## Job Card Verification

For each job card, check:

- Check the Berklee Online Careers in Music role directory when researching any job card. Use it as one comparison source for job duties, title overlap, adjacent roles, career paths, and wording gaps.
- If Berklee Online has the same role, compare its information against the T.R.A.C.K card before proposing edits.
- If Berklee Online does not have the same role, check whether it has a similar or parent role and note the distinction.
- Do not copy Berklee Online wording. Use it only to verify facts, identify differences, and rewrite the card in T.R.A.C.K's voice.
- The job title is real and current enough to be useful.
- The job is not just a task inside another stronger job title.
- The description starts with what the person actually does.
- The responsibilities match real postings or industry descriptions.
- The starting point matches realistic entry routes.
- The career steps move in a believable order.
- The timeline does not promise speed or certainty.
- Required licenses, credentials, degrees, union pathways, or certifications are named when they matter.
- Portfolio-based, audition-based, freelance, location-heavy, or relationship-based hiring is described plainly when relevant.

## Program Verification

For each school or training provider listed on a job card, confirm:

- The program exists now.
- The program name is correct.
- The credential level is correct.
- The program category is correct: U.S. Programs or Programs Outside the U.S.
- The program type label is correct, such as College / University, Arts / Conservatory, Specialized / Technical, Graduate Level, Certificate / Associate, or Online.
- The description matches the actual program.
- The program teaches skills connected to the job.
- The program is not only a conference, association membership, networking group, or short career-development event.
- If the program is outside the U.S., language of instruction is included when available.
- If the program requires a previous degree or professional background, that is reflected in the description.
- When editing a job card, review every listed program description on that card, not only new program additions or removals.
- Program descriptions must describe verified curriculum, degree names, certificates, departments, or training routes.
- Do not add advisory sentences that tell students what they should do beyond the program unless the user specifically asks for that note.
- Do not use comparative fit claims such as "strongest for," "best for," "ideal for," or "direct fit."
- Do not use "prepares students" or "trains students" in program descriptions. Use "covers," "teaches," "includes," or "provides training."
- Do not add explanatory fit sentences such as "The connection," "The program connects," "These courses connect," or "The connection to." State the verified program content directly.

## Certification And Career Development Verification

For each certification or career-development item, confirm:

- It is a real credential, training, course, workshop, license, exam, union pathway, or career-development resource.
- It is placed under Certifications and Career Development, not Programs, unless it is part of a true school or training-provider program.
- It is useful for the specific job, not just vaguely related to the field.
- The name is accurate.
- A hyperlink is included when a stable official page exists.
- Duplicate items are removed.
- Vague items like "camera," "lighting," "business," or "networking" are replaced with specific training options or removed.

## Research Depth By Risk

Use deeper checking when:

- The job requires a license, clinical credential, teaching credential, union pathway, safety credential, or legal authority.
- The program is expensive, degree-granting, graduate-level, or international.
- The job title sounds niche, outdated, or possibly folded into another job.
- A school has only one or two job matches.
- A job has fewer than three program listings.
- A program appears on many unrelated job cards.
- A claim mentions accreditation, rankings, official recognition, job outcomes, or exclusive status.

## Evidence Rules

- Keep enough notes to explain why a change was made.
- Record the source name and URL for every added school, certification, or major factual correction.
- Do not quote long passages from sources.
- Paraphrase in T.R.A.C.K voice after verifying the fact.
- If sources conflict, prefer official program pages and current employer postings.
- If a source does not prove the claim, do not use it as proof.
- If the evidence is unclear, keep the wording cautious or leave the item out.

## Writing After Research

After research is complete:

- Apply the T.R.A.C.K writing style rules.
- Remove em dashes.
- Remove banned AI words and AI expression patterns.
- Remove editorial comments.
- Keep language direct, specific, and human.
- Do not add rankings or claims that one school is better than another.
- Do not promise employment.
- Do not overstate a program fit.
- Remove advisory phrases such as "requires focused practice," "requires additional focused experience," "students should," and "would need" unless the user has specifically approved that wording.
- If the fit is adjacent, describe only the verified program content and leave out opinion about what is missing.

## Final Research Checklist

Before a job card edit is accepted:

1. The job title has been checked against real usage.
2. The job is either kept as a real role or consolidated into a stronger parent role.
3. The description matches real duties.
4. The responsibilities match real duties.
5. The starting point is realistic.
6. The career steps match how people commonly enter or move through the work.
7. Every listed program has a valid source.
8. Every listed program fits the job.
9. Every certification or career-development item is specific and useful.
10. Conferences, associations, and short workshops are not listed as Programs unless they are true training programs.
11. International programs include language information when available.
12. No source is being stretched beyond what it actually says.
13. The final copy follows the T.R.A.C.K writing style rules.
14. Every listed program description for the edited card has been reviewed.
15. Program copy avoids advisory, comparative, or ranking language.
